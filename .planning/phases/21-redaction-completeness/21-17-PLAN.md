<objective>
Close **W-06** and **IN-01** of `21-REVIEW-2.md`.

`factoredKeyVocabularyMatchesItsReadableSpecification` is the **only** guard on a hand-optimised,
security-critical regex rewrite, so its strength matters more than most. The reviewer verified the
factoring itself is correct — a differential fuzz of 400 000 random keys across three consumer
contexts, 500 000 additional camelCase-heavy keys, and an exhaustive sweep of every key of length ≤ 4
over a 14-character alphabet produced **zero divergences**. **This is a finding about the guard, not
about the code it guards.** Nothing in this plan may change the shipped vocabulary or its
classification of any key.

The guard has two structural weaknesses and one arithmetic one:

1. **Asymmetric comparison.** The naive side runs a single rule; the shipped side runs
   `redactWith(doc, STRICT)`, i.e. the entire pipeline — `authHeaderRegex`, `bearerRegex`,
   `basicAuthRegex`, `jwtRegex`, `urlTokenParamRegex`, `formBodyParamRegex`, both cookie rules and any
   custom pattern. Any of those firing sets `shippedRedacted = true` and **masks an under-match in the
   factored form**. That is this phase's own test-vacuity question — what else in the pipeline would
   catch this input? — and the answer here is "eight other rules". For the current corpus the masking
   risk happens to be nil, but that is a property of the fixture, not of the test.
2. **One consumer context.** Only `jsonSecretKeyRegex` is exercised. `urlTokenParamRegex` and
   `formBodyParamRegex` embed the same expression with different anchors, and `formBodyParamRegex`
   additionally captures the key in group 2, so a factoring change that altered group numbering would
   corrupt its `$1$2=[REDACTED]` replacement without failing here. Group counts are 2 and 2 today, so
   Pitfall 7 holds — but the test does not check it.
3. `assertTrue(corpus.size > 100, …)` measures the list **before** `.distinct()`, so it does not bound
   what is actually iterated.

**IN-01** — `NAIVE_KEY_EXPR_FOR_TEST` is `internal const val`, which compiles to a
`public static final String` field on `Redaction`. A ~700-character test seam therefore ships inside
`Custom-AI-Agent-<version>.jar` and is reachable by any code on the classpath. It is inert and
harmless, but the file's two other test seams are `internal fun`s, which at least get name-mangled,
and the BApp Store review surface is a live concern for this project.

Locked decisions in force: **D-11** (token-boundary containment), **D-12** (no benign-key denylist),
**D-13** (camelCase ships with three accepted false positives), and the maintainer's WR-01 ruling of
2026-08-12 narrowing `key`/`code` to credential-bearing contexts. The `token`-vocabulary question
(`token_type`, `tokenType`, `session_count`, `auth_type`, `auth_url`) is a **separate, open maintainer
decision** and is explicitly out of scope — those five stay pinned as accepted over-redactions.
