<objective>
Land the actual SEC-04 fix: move the access-control checks out of the dead `intercept(ApplicationCallPipeline.Call)`
block registered after `routing{}` and into a Ktor `ApplicationPlugin` whose `onCall` hook attaches to the
`Plugins` phase — which is fixed at pipeline construction and therefore order-independent by design.

This is deliberately not a line-reordering fix. `ApplicationCallPipeline` is constructed with the phases
`Setup, Monitoring, Plugins, Call, Fallback` in that literal order, and `PluginBuilder.onCall` is hard-wired
to `Plugins`; so the class of bug being fixed cannot recur through a future re-ordering of the
`embeddedServer` module lambda. Both facts were verified by decompiling `ktor-server-core-jvm-3.1.3` and by
executing probes against a real Netty server (`20-RESEARCH.md` sections "Pipeline Mechanics" and "Decision 1").

This plan also WIRES the Wave 1 `McpBlockedRequestReporter`. That wiring is load-bearing, not cosmetic:
four locked decisions (D-06 audit event, D-07 sanitization, D-09 rate-limited Output line, D-10 hashing)
have no effect at all unless `onBlocked` actually calls `reporter.report`. An `onBlocked = { }` compiles,
passes detekt and passes every other check in this phase — which is exactly why it is pinned by two
`key_links` and by grep-level acceptance criteria in both tasks below, and asserted end-to-end in
`20-04`'s `McpAccessControlPipelineTest`.

Purpose: SEC-04 (critical, Finding 1) plus the SEC-05 5a/5b/5c sub-items that live in the same file.
Output: `McpAccessControlPlugin.kt`, a rewritten security section of `KtorMcpServerManager.kt`, and the
extended predicate test class.
</objective>
