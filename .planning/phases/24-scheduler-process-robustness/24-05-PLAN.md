<objective>
Close the second half of SC6 (REL-07): `App.workerPool` becomes bounded and named.

The order is the whole plan. `workerPool` has exactly two submit sites. `AgentSupervisor.kt:1013`
submits a short, bursty auto-restart task. `AgentSupervisor.kt:1037` submits a stdout pump that calls
`reader.forEachLine { … }` and therefore occupies its worker thread for the **entire lifetime of the
service process**. `startService` is reachable from exactly two call sites — `ollama-serve` and
`lmstudio-server` — so at most two worker threads can be permanently occupied.

Bounding first is the failure. A fixed pool sized at or below two deadlocks the auto-restart path
outright: both threads park in `forEachLine`, restart tasks queue forever on an unbounded queue, and
nothing reports it. A bounded queue with a throwing rejection policy is worse — the submit at
`AgentSupervisor.kt:1013` is not inside a try, so a rejection kills that call path. And
`CallerRunsPolicy` is actively dangerous here: it would run the infinite pump inline on the calling
thread, which risks re-introducing an EDT block through the back door immediately after Phase 23
(REL-05) finished moving work off the EDT.

So: **separate the long-lived pump, then bound, then name.**

Purpose: an unbounded worker pool stops being a way to spawn threads without limit, without trading
that for a stalled extension.
Output: the pump on its own named daemon thread, a bounded named `workerPool`, and a headless suite
pinning all three properties.
</objective>

  not a rejection.
